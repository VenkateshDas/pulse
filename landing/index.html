What you can do with Pulse today

Included in the downloadable beta. Some readings and actions depend on your hardware and macOS permissions.

Know what needs attention

Home brings together processor, memory, disk and temperature readings with plain-language condition summaries. Inspect busy apps and GPU activity where readings are available.

See where your space goes

Browse sized folders in Files, inspect storage visually and find large items. Hidden-space insights highlight backups, old downloads, developer data and AI-tool caches.

Clean up with a review first

Review caches, logs and other cleanup candidates before selected files move to Trash. Safety grades and usage evidence help you decide; they cannot guarantee a file is disposable.

Find exact duplicates

Compare file contents to find matching copies, then review what to keep. Clone and hard-link checks help distinguish logical file size from potential space savings.

Remove apps and related files

Search installed apps and review confidence-graded preferences, caches and helpers. Scan for leftovers from removed apps, with protection for system apps and a receipt for actual results.

Understand battery and energy

See charge, condition, cycles, capacity trends and recorded battery sessions. Seven-day app energy shares are estimates based on processor activity. Supported Bluetooth devices show battery readings too.

Check your connection

See Wi-Fi and connection readings, live traffic and previous speed estimates. Run a bounded HTTPS download/upload test when needed, or opt into automatic tests. Network-name access is optional.

Control each display

Use named brightness sliders for built-in and supported external displays. Match brightness, route physical keys and dim further in software. Hardware control depends on your monitor and connection.

Make the menu bar useful

Hide and reveal icons with a chevron or auto-hide timer. Choose CPU, memory, temperature and battery readings beside Pulse; open the popover for display, network and sleep controls.

Keep your Mac awake

Prevent system or display sleep for a chosen duration, or until you turn it off. Control it from the menu bar or a keyboard shortcut during downloads, presentations and renders.

Follow changes over time

Activity records observed events and sustained process anomalies. Storage Changes compares recorded observations to show folder growth. History builds while Pulse observes; missing periods stay gaps.

Ask Pulse for help

Ask questions about your Mac using your chosen AI provider, model and API key. Review consequential actions before they run. Questions and requested diagnostic results go to that provider; provider charges may apply.

More detail when you need it

Advanced tools are a disclosure away. Open the details without changing your everyday navigation.

App activity and investigations

Group processes by app, inspect processor and memory use, view process history and measure a slowdown. Send Quit asks an eligible app to close normally; it may prompt to save work.

Startup helpers

Review supported launch helpers and manage eligible user-owned entries. Changes affect the next sign-in; global entries are read only, and this is not a complete list of login apps.

Maintenance you can inspect

Review supported native tasks and their results. Saved-window-state cleanup skips active apps. Operations that are unavailable are labeled with macOS guidance.

Performance tests and diagnostics

Start explicit processor, memory and storage tests, or inspect native diagnostic evidence. Tests temporarily use resources; reference scores are synthetic comparisons, not a hardware health rating.

Keyboard shortcuts and native CLI

Use the command palette to navigate and find actions. A bundled native command-line tool and portable agent skill expose observation, diagnostics and reviewed actions for technical workflows.

Preferences that fit your Mac

Choose light or dark appearance, Larger text, launch at login, notifications and menu-bar readings. Keyboard navigation and update checks keep everyday use within reach.

  • Without Pulse

    The "System Data" Black Hole

    97 GB of "System Data" you can't click, can't understand, and can't delete. Completely blind.

    With Pulse

    Explore Folders by Size

    Browse folder sizes and inspect caches, backups and other large items. Review selected paths before moving them to Trash.

  • Without Pulse

    The Mystery Slowdown

    Mac sounds like a jet engine. Activity Monitor shows hundreds of cryptic processes. Good luck finding the culprit.

    With Pulse

    Inspect the Evidence

    Inspect busy apps and process evidence before choosing an action. Send Quit asks eligible apps to close normally and respects save prompts.

  • Without Pulse

    The Illusion of Uninstalling

    Drag to Trash. Done? No. Gigabytes of preference files, caches, and launch agents left behind. Invisible.

    With Pulse

    Reviewed App Removal

    Scan installed apps or orphan leftovers, review confidence grades, then move selected items to Trash. Some related files may remain.

  • Without Pulse

    The Fear of Deleting

    Too afraid to clean manually. What if you break macOS? What if something important vanishes? So nothing gets cleaned.

    With Pulse

    A Record of Trash Moves

    Review recorded Trash moves in the undo journal. Restore while items remain available; emptying Trash cannot be undone.

  • Without Pulse

    The Dead Brightness Key

    Plug in an external monitor and your brightness keys stop working. Crouch behind the screen, poke tiny hardware buttons, navigate a 1990s on-screen menu.

    With Pulse

    One Slider Per Display

    Use brightness keys and per-display sliders on supported displays, with software dimming when needed. Hardware behavior varies with the display and connection.

  • Without Pulse

    The Overflowing Menu Bar

    A dozen icons crammed across the top, some of them swallowed by the notch. The one you actually need is never where you left it.

    With Pulse

    A Menu Bar You Control

    Push the icons you rarely touch behind a chevron. Click to bring them back when you need them. Or set a timer and let them tuck away on their own. And show the numbers you actually care about — any mix of CPU, memory, temperature, and battery — right in the bar.

  • Without Pulse

    The Overnight Job That Wasn't

    Start a render, an export, a big upload, then walk away. The Mac sleeps, the job stalls, and you come back to nothing done. Caffeine widgets you forget to turn off don't help either.

    With Pulse

    Awake for the Duration You Choose

    Flip Keep Awake before a long render or export and the Mac won't sleep mid-job, display or system. Set a duration up to 8 hours and it turns itself back off, or leave it indefinite and flip it off yourself.
